About Satellite Orbit Tracker

This app tracks satellites in real-time using publicly available orbital data.

What is a TLE?

A Two-Line Element set (TLE) is a standardized format for describing a satellite's orbit. It contains numbers like inclination, eccentricity, and mean motion that together define the shape, orientation, and timing of the orbit. TLEs are published by organisations like CelesTrak and updated regularly.

What is SGP4?

SGP4 (Simplified General Perturbations 4) is the mathematical model used to predict where a satellite will be at any given time, based on its TLE. It accounts for Earth's oblateness, atmospheric drag, and gravitational effects from the Sun and Moon. This app uses Skyfield, which implements SGP4 internally.

What is the ISS?

The International Space Station (ISS) is a modular space station in low Earth orbit (~420 km altitude). It orbits Earth roughly every 92 minutes at about 7.66 km/s. It has been continuously occupied since November 2000 and is a collaboration between NASA, Roscosmos, JAXA, ESA, and CSA.

How This App Works

1. Fetch the latest TLE from CelesTrak
2. Feed it to Skyfield's SGP4 propagator
3. Get latitude, longitude, altitude at any time
4. Display on a 3D globe with globe.gl

Accuracy

TLE-based predictions are most accurate within a few days of the TLE epoch. After 1–2 weeks, positional error can grow to tens of kilometers. For precise tracking, fresh TLEs should be used.
